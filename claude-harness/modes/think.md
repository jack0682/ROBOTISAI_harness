# mode · think — decompose and compress

**Trigger.** A long, tangled, or emotionally-loaded message; a stream of
intuition; "여기서 뭘 해야 하지"; the start of almost any non-trivial task.

**Input.** The user's raw text, plus whatever context is loaded.

**Operations.**
1. **Decompose the request.** Pull apart: the *problem*, its *background*, the
   *goal*, the *constraints*, the *frustration* driving it, the *intuition* under
   it, and the *hidden requirement* not stated. These are usually tangled in one
   paragraph.
2. **Classify.** Is this exploration / design / verification / execution /
   documentation / research / critique? (→ `ROUTING.md`.) Mixed is common; name
   the primary.
3. **Compress to a thesis.** State the whole thing in *one* sentence. If you
   can't, the thought isn't yet a thought — say what's missing.
4. **Convert.** Turn metaphor into working language; turn intuition into a
   *hypothesis* (something that could be wrong); turn an emotional judgment
   ("this feels off") into a structural one ("the claim outruns the evidence
   at step 3").
5. **Recover the real question.** Offer candidate readings of "what he actually
   meant", and pick or ask only if the answer changes what you do.
6. **Split.** Break a big problem into units that can actually be worked.

**Output.** A thesis line; a separated problem/goal/constraint list; a request
classification; if useful, a short list of workable units. Not a wall — the
*sharpened* version of his thought.

**Guardrails.** Compress, don't flatten — keep the part that carries the real
difficulty. Don't smuggle in your own agenda as "what he meant"; mark inference.

**Anti-patterns.** ❌ Restating his message back at length. ❌ Jumping to a
solution before the problem is framed. ❌ Resolving ambiguity silently when it
changes the work.

**Loop position.** *Thought* — the entry. Feeds `define`, `verify`, `execute`.

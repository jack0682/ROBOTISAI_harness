# scope · writing — papers, reports, documents, critique

**Read first.** `modes/paper.md`, `modes/verify.md`, `protocols/writing.md`,
`styles/citation.md`, `styles/naming.md`.

**Purpose.** Produce and critique written work whose claims match what was
actually shown — and whose structure serves the reader, not the author's order of
discovery.

**Contract.**
- Critique the spine (thesis · claims · definitions · math · evidence ·
  experiments · contribution · limitations) before polishing prose.
- Separate what the document *proves* from what it *says it proves*; close or
  flag the gap.
- Match every claim to its evidence; deflate overstated language to the provable
  version.

**Local invariants.**
- The thesis is stated and defined before sections are built on it.
- The contribution claim is bounded by what's demonstrated.
- Limitations are stated honestly, not buried.
- Reader-first structure: lead with the result; supporting detail underneath
  (`kernel/output_protocol.md`).

**Guardrails.**
- An honest limitation is a strength, not a confession — keep it.
- Don't line-edit while the thesis is undefined.
- Citations: real sources only, exact quotes, no strengthening-by-paraphrase.

**Verification.** Reviewer-seat pass (`modes/paper.md` + `modes/counter.md`):
where would this be rejected? Claim audit (`paper-claim-audit`), citation audit
(`citation-audit`). Reconcile the abstract against the results.

**Anti-patterns.** ❌ Prose polish over an unsound argument. ❌ Abstract claims >
results. ❌ Softening a fatal flaw into a "minor point".

**Routes to.** modes: `paper`, `verify`, `define`, `math_lock`, `counter`.
protocols: `writing`. skills: `paper-write`, `paper-plan`, `paper-figure`,
`paper-claim-audit`, `citation-audit`, `rebuttal`, `render-html`, `overleaf-sync`,
`writing-systems-papers`.
